# Business Findings — UCI Online Retail II

## Executive summary

The full workbook contains 1,067,371 transaction lines from 1 December 2009 to 9 December 2011. After the documented sale-line rules, 1,041,670 lines remain as valid sales. The analysis identifies £20,972,594.57 in revenue across 40,077 invoices. Customer-level metrics are calculated only where Customer ID is present.

The United Kingdom accounts for approximately 85.2% of measured valid-sale revenue. This concentration suggests that country-level comparisons should show both absolute revenue and revenue share, and that international expansion conclusions should account for the dataset's home-market concentration.

The repeat-customer rate is calculated among customers with an identified Customer ID and at least one valid sale. It is descriptive, not a causal estimate of loyalty.

## Inactivity model — chronological holdout

The prediction target is an operational proxy: a customer is labelled inactive when they make no purchase in the 90 days after a monthly observation date. Features use the preceding 180 days. Snapshots without a complete future label window are excluded. The holdout is chronological, with a cutoff of 2011-05-31.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Customer-month snapshots | 51,003 |
| Training snapshots | 37,136 |
| Test snapshots | 13,867 |
| Test inactivity prevalence | 44.96% |
| Logistic Regression F1 (inactive class) | 0.675 |
| Logistic Regression recall (inactive class) | 0.795 |
| Logistic Regression ROC-AUC | 0.728 |
| Logistic Regression PR-AUC | 0.640 |
| Random Forest F1 (inactive class) | 0.661 |
| Random Forest recall (inactive class) | 0.748 |
| Random Forest ROC-AUC | 0.715 |
| Random Forest PR-AUC | 0.624 |

Logistic Regression is the stronger of these two tested baselines on the reported holdout metrics. Its inactive-class recall of 79.5% comes with precision of 58.6%, so a meaningful share of customers flagged as at risk would not meet the later inactivity definition. Threshold selection should be based on the retailer's campaign cost, contact capacity, and the relative cost of missing an at-risk customer.

These are baseline results, not proof of production readiness. The dataset is historical, the target is a 90-day inactivity proxy rather than permanent churn, and snapshots from the same customer can occur in both periods. Before operational use, test a customer-grouped or strictly forward validation design, calibrate probabilities, evaluate threshold economics, and check stability across time and customer cohorts.

## Recommended business actions

1. **Prioritize retention experiments.** Use the Logistic Regression score to rank customers for a small, controlled win-back test. Compare incremental repeat purchase and margin against a randomized holdout; do not assume that model scores imply treatment response.
2. **Build country-specific plans.** UK concentration is high. Track non-UK markets separately and evaluate growth by revenue, order frequency, and average order value rather than revenue alone.
3. **Improve customer identity coverage.** 243,007 rows lack Customer ID. Better identity capture would improve repeat-purchase, cohort, and customer-risk analysis.
4. **Monitor cancellation and return behaviour.** Keep cancellations and non-sale lines separate from valid sales in business reporting.
5. **Use cohort retention with context.** Compare cohorts at the same age (month since first purchase); newer cohorts have shorter observable histories.
